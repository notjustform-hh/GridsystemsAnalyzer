# Layout Specification: Magazine Editorial Spread - Artwork Showcase Format

## 1. Format & Proportions
- **Orientation**: Landscape double-page spread
- **Aspect Ratio**: 1.46:1
- **Page Ratio vs φ**: 1.46 (φ = 1.618) ✓ 90% proximity to golden ratio
- **Assumed Format**: Art & culture magazine spread, likely A3 or large format publication
- **Spread or Single**: Double-page spread with minimal central spine and symmetrical layout structure

## 2. Margins
- **Top**: 40px (3.1%)
- **Right/Outer**: 40px (2.1% per page)
- **Bottom**: 40px (3.1%)
- **Left/Inner**: 40px (2.1% per page)
- **Gutter**: 40px (2.1%) minimal central spine
- **Symmetry**: Symmetrical margins with organized content zones
- **Notes**: Consistent 40px margins provide clean professional frame for dense editorial content

## 3. Grid System
- **Grid Type**: Systematic multi-column editorial layout with integrated gallery showcase
- **Column Count per Page**: 4 columns per page (8 total across spread)
- **Total Columns (spread)**: 8 text columns + central gallery zone + timeline area
- **Column Widths**: Text columns: ~200px each | Gallery spans: 270px modules | Timeline: full width
- **Column Width (px)**: Text columns: 200px | Gallery modules: 270px | Timeline: 1680px
- **Gutter Width**: 20px between text columns, 40px between major zones
- **Row/Zone Structure**: Header(40px) | Text area(480px) | Gallery(420px) | Timeline(120px)
- **Baseline Grid**: Academic publication standard with systematic spacing
- **Compound Grid**: Yes - text columns overlaid with gallery modules and timeline integration
- **Confidence**: Very High
- **Reasoning**: Clear systematic organization with distinct zones for text, visual content, and temporal data

## 9. Responsive Adaptation Notes
The dense multi-column structure and integrated gallery system will face significant challenges on mobile devices. The 8-column text layout will collapse to single-column format, eliminating the sophisticated information architecture that defines academic publication design. The artwork gallery showcasing 5-6 images in systematic modules will become a vertical image stack, losing the comparative viewing experience essential for art criticism and analysis. The timeline chart providing historical context will require horizontal scrolling or complete restructuring, breaking the temporal relationships that support the editorial content. Most critically, the academic publication's high information density - with extensive text, visual materials, and reference data integrated across zones - will be reduced to sequential mobile content blocks. The scholarly approach that balances text analysis with visual evidence through systematic layout will be compromised. The bibliography and reference materials may become disconnected from their related content. Consider creating a simplified mobile version that maintains the text-to-image relationships through clear sectioning, while accepting that the sophisticated academic layout's simultaneous content presentation will become sequential. The timeline should be redesigned as an interactive mobile element to preserve its contextual value.
